His AI agents kept 40,000 small businesses running through the 2034 blackout

By Lina Haddad, in San Francisco

When the regional grid failed for eleven days last October, most companies stopped answering their phones. The clients of Loop Labs did not. Their invoices went out, their orders were confirmed and their customers were called back — by software that had been designed, from day one, to work without a stable connection.

"I grew up with power cuts. I built a company that assumes the lights will go off," says Adam Barbir, 25, who founded Loop Labs in 2032, straight out of MIT and Polytechnique. This week he was named in the Forbes 30 Under 30 list, and Loop Labs opened its second office, in Paris.

The agents, he insists, are not magic: "They just do the boring parts, every day, without forgetting. That's all most businesses need."
